History of Braille

Louis Braille was born in 1809 in Coupvray, France. He lost his sight as a child due to an accident, an experience that inspired him to develop a system allowing people with visual impairments to read and write independently.


At just fifteen years old he created the Braille system, based on a cell of six raised dots. Thanks to this innovation, millions of people around the world have been able to access education, culture, and communication without depending on others.

How does Braille work?

The Braille system uses a cell made up of six dots arranged in two columns of three dots each.


Depending on which dots are raised, it represents letters, numbers, punctuation marks, mathematical symbols, and even sheet music.


Reading is done using the sense of touch, gently sliding the fingers over each cell.

Where is Braille used?

The Braille system is part of daily life for millions of people with visual impairments. Thanks to it, they can access important information, study, work, and get around with greater independence.


  • Books, magazines, and libraries.
  • Schools, universities, and educational centers.
  • Medication packaging and labels.
  • Hospitals and health centers.
  • Train stations, subways, and public transport.
  • Room signs, elevators, and buildings.
  • ATMs and payment terminals.
  • Computers and devices with Braille displays.
  • Adapted sheet music.
  • Election materials and official documents.

Fun Facts

Inventor

Louis Braille created this system when he was only 15 years old.

Worldwide

Braille is used in more than a hundred countries around the world.

Music

There is a special Braille system for reading and writing sheet music.

Mathematics

It can also represent mathematical operations, scientific formulas, and programming.
